This project compares neural networks for predicting whether a student passes
the Math course. A final grade (G3) of 10 or higher is labeled as a pass; a
grade below 10 is labeled as a fail. The Math course dataset contains 395
students. The data was split into training and test sets, using 80% for
training and 20% for testing.

Results
Across the eight settings tested for each activation function, tanh had the
highest average test accuracy (84.3%). Sigmoid averaged 83.4%, while ReLU
averaged 81.3%. The best individual run was sigmoid with a learning rate of
0.10, 200 epochs, and 3 hidden layers; its test accuracy was 89.9%. Using 
average test accuracy as the main measure, although tanh did not win on 
every metric; it was the most consistent choice by average test accuracy 
across the settings we tested so it performed best.

Limitations
Most models reached nearly 100% training accuracy, but their test accuracy was
lower. This suggests overfitting: the models fit the training data better than
they generalize to unseen students.
